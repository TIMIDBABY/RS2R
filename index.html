Abstract

Underactuated dexterous hands are attractive for robotic manipulation because of their low cost and ease of deployment. Their effective use in real-world tasks, however, requires not only grasp diversity, but also hardware feasibility and robustness to physical disturbances. This paper presents RS2R, a physically executable Sim2Real workflow for stable grasping with underactuated dexterous hands. Two coupled challenges in deployment are addressed: the kinematic feasibility gap caused by mechanical joint coupling, and the perception--dynamics gap introduced by sensing noise and physical mismatch. To bridge these gaps, grasp synthesis is performed directly in the reduced joint space of the target hand, so that mechanically feasible grasps can be generated and further filtered through dynamic stability tests. A student policy is then trained under noisy observations via asymmetric distillation, while a persistent kinematic prior is used to regularize learning toward the same physically stable grasp manifold. In this way, grasp generation and dynamic execution are unified under a shared hardware-feasible prior. Experiments in simulation and on a UR5-InspireHand platform demonstrate strong physical validity and robust zero-shot Sim2Real transfer under latency, friction, and pose perturbations.
